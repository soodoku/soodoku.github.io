Measuring Learning in Informative Processes
With Robert Luskin and Ariel Helfer.
🗃Replication Materials
Observed knowledge gain can understate learning when those who know more also learn more and easy questions limit their room to improve. The paper derives conditions under which post-process knowledge is more closely associated with true learning. It performs better in proportional-learning simulations matched to 21 Deliberative Polls, but observed gain does better in catch-up simulations and most split-item comparisons. Scoring and baseline adjustment also change associations with education and attitude change.
