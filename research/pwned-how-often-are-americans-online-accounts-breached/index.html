Pwned: The Risk of Exposure From Data Breaches
With Ken Cor.
ACM Web Science Conference, 289–293, 2019.
🗃Replication Materials · Supporting InformationRelated: Bob Rudis Analyzes Exposure by Breach; I Have Been Pwned: Evidence from the Florida Voter Registration Data
We looked up one email address for each of 5,000 U.S. adults in the July 2018 Have I Been Pwned database. At least one record appeared for 82.84% of the sample. After excluding spam lists, 71.78% still had a recorded exposure. Both figures describe the email address we queried for each person.

Which exposures count?

Both rows use all 5,000 people. Each breach record pairs one person’s email with one breach. Repeated copies of the same record count once.

Of the 5,000 people, 858 had no record, 553 appeared only on spam lists, and 3,589 appeared in at least one nonspam breach. Among those 3,589 people, there were 2.59 nonspam breaches per person.

The 2019 paper reported 2.10 nonspam breaches per person. Its calculation kept the 858 people with no records but dropped the 553 with only spam-list records. Restoring their zeros lowers the mean to 1.866. Removing 26 duplicate nonspam records brings it to 1.861. The reported share with any exposure, 82.84%, is the same in both versions.
