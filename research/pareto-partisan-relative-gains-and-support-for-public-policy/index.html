Pareto Partisan? Relative Gains and Support for Public Policy
With Alexander G. Theodoridis.
🗃Replication Materials
If people prefer more for both sides to less for both, everyone should select the plan giving both sides more. In a 2018 CCES highway choice, only 35.0% of Democrats and 27.1% of Republicans did so; the rest chose smaller allocations that left their side ahead. In a post-election experiment, raising opponents’ income gain from 3% to 7%, while keeping the own-party gain at 5%, changed policy support by -20.0 points on a 0–100 scale (95% CI [-23.9, -16.1]). Relative partisan gains affect support, but the exercises do not distinguish spite from dislike of falling behind; the highway comparison also changes total spending.
